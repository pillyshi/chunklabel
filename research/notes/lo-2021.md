# Notes: lo-2021

> Lo, K., Jin, Y., Tan, W., Liu, M., Du, L., & Buntine, W. (2021). Transformer over
> pre-trained transformer for neural text segmentation with enhanced topic coherence.
> *Findings of EMNLP 2021*, pp. 3334–3340.

## Summary

Transformer² stacks two Transformer layers: a fixed pre-trained sentence encoder at
the bottom and a trainable segmentation classifier at the top. The key contribution is
using a *multi-task loss* that jointly predicts segment boundaries and topic labels,
forcing the upper layer to learn topic coherence between sentences.

## Architecture

**Bottom level (fixed):** Pre-trained Transformer (BERT, XLNet, RoBERTa, ALBERT)
used as sentence encoder. Two types of embeddings are concatenated:
- `S_single`: [CLS] embedding from masked-language-model pretraining (local sentence info)
- `S_pairwise`: [CLS] embedding from next-sentence prediction or sentence-order
  prediction pretraining (between-sentence coherence info)

**Upper level (trainable):** A 5-layer Transformer with 24 attention heads and
1024-dim feedforward layers trained on the concatenated sentence embeddings.

**Loss:**
```
L = L_seg(boundary classification) + L_topic(topic label classification)
```
L_topic is auxiliary — it forces the model to learn that sentences within the same
segment share a topic label, improving coherence learning.

**Training trick:** 70% of inner sentences randomly masked during training to address
class imbalance (boundaries are rare). Beginning sentences are never masked.

## Datasets and results (WikiSection)

38,000 Wikipedia articles, English and German, Disease and City domains.
4 sub-datasets: en_disease (27 subtopics), en_city (30), de_disease (25), de_city (27).

| Model | en_disease | en_city |
|-------|------------|---------|
| TextTiling (baseline) | 37.4 | 36.8 |
| S-LSTM (prior SOTA) | 20.0 | 9.1 |
| Transformer²_BERT | **18.8** | **9.1** |
| Transformer²_de_BERT | 16.0 | 7.3 |

(Lower Pk is better.)

**Ablation findings:**
- Removing L_topic (auxiliary topic loss): +1.6 Pk on en_disease → topic prediction
  helps boundary accuracy.
- Removing S_pairwise: +0.4 Pk → pairwise coherence embeddings contribute.
- Language-specific BERT (German) outperforms domain-specific (BioClinical BERT)
  → tokenization quality dominates over domain alignment.

## Key findings relevant to chunklabel

**Multi-task framing: chunklabel does this implicitly.** The LLM is asked to output
both a verbatim quote (span extraction) and a category (topic label) in a single call.
This is structurally analogous to Transformer²'s joint L_seg + L_topic objective.
The question is whether forcing the model to predict topic labels jointly improves
boundary quality — the ablation says yes (+8% relative).

**Topic coherence as a training signal.** The auxiliary topic loss enforces that
sentences within a segment should have the same topic label. For chunklabel, the
Normalizer already clusters categories post-hoc. Doing this *before* or *during*
segmentation (e.g. prompting the LLM to ensure each chunk is topically homogeneous)
could improve output quality.

**Two-pass motivation.** The two-level architecture directly motivates the
two-pass coherence merge idea (see `ideas/two-pass-coherence-merge.md`):
1st pass = chunklabel's `split()` (boundary placement);
2nd pass = embed categories + cluster + merge adjacent same-cluster chunks.

**WikiSection is the right benchmark dataset.** It has 38,000 documents with
ground-truth segment boundaries and topic labels — ideal for evaluating both
boundary placement (Pk / WD) and category naming quality.

**Language-specific embeddings > domain-specific.** For `boundary_score` computation
in chunklabel, a general language model (e.g. `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) is likely better
than a domain-tuned model, unless the domain is highly specialized.

## Limitations

- WikiSection is restricted to Wikipedia disease and city articles; generalization to
  other domains is unvalidated.
- The model is supervised — requires large annotated corpora. chunklabel is zero-shot.
- Combining S_single and S_pairwise does not consistently help (Table 5): on en_city
  and de_city, removing S_single *improves* results.
