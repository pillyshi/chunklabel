# Notes: zhao-2024

> Zhao, J., Ji, Z., Feng, Y., Qi, P., Niu, S., Tang, B., Xiong, F., Li, Z. (2024).
> Meta-Chunking: Learning Text Segmentation and Semantic Completion via Logical
> Perception. arXiv:2410.12788 (read: v3, May 2025).
> Code: https://github.com/IAAR-Shanghai/Meta-Chunking

## Summary

Proposes two LLM-uncertainty-based boundary detectors for RAG chunking, plus a
dynamic merge step and an (LLM-rewriting) "semantic completion" stage. Only the
first part is relevant to chunklabel: **Perplexity (PPL) Chunking** places
boundaries using a per-sentence perplexity series computed by a small LM, with
no generation and no prompt.

## Method (PPL Chunking, §3.1.1)

1. Split text into sentences `x_1..x_n`.
2. For each sentence compute `PPL_M(x_i)` = mean over its tokens of the token
   PPL conditioned on *all preceding text* (`t_<i`), i.e. a sentence-level
   average of `-log p(t | context)`.
3. Treat `PPL_seq = (PPL(x_1), ..., PPL(x_n))` as a 1-D series and take
   **local minima** as candidate boundaries:
   - both neighbours higher and at least one side's difference > θ, or
   - left difference > θ and right neighbour equal.
4. For long inputs, KV cache is truncated from the left once memory or context
   limit is reached.
5. Dynamic merging: adjacent meta-chunks are greedily merged up to a
   user-specified length `L`.

Note the sign: boundaries are at PPL **minima**, not peaks. Appendix B / Fig. 4
argues that sentences within a logical unit show a gradually *declining* PPL
(each sentence is increasingly predictable given the unit so far), so a
minimum marks the *end* of a unit, after which PPL jumps up again. This is a
"split after the trough" rule rather than "split at the spike".

MSP (Margin Sampling) Chunking is the prompt-based alternative: yes/no token
probability margin for "should sentence1+sentence2 be split?". It needs a
prompt and an instruction model, so it is outside the "no prompting" scope.

## Results

- LongBench QA F1 (Table 1): PPL Chunking with **Qwen2-0.5B** is competitive
  with LumberChunker (Qwen2.5-14B) and similarity chunking, at a fraction of the
  time (e.g. 2WikiMQA 13.56 F1 / 141 s vs. LumberChunker 13.28 / 5245 s).
- Table 3: PPL Chunking works as well or better with **base** models than
  instruction models — it relies on LM likelihood, not instruction following.
- Evaluation is purely downstream RAG QA (F1, BLEU, ROUGE). No Pk/WindowDiff on
  segmentation benchmarks, so boundary accuracy itself is not measured.

## Key findings relevant to chunklabel

- **Existence proof** that a 0.5B causal LM's surprisal series is a usable
  boundary signal without any prompt. Boundaries are sentence indices, so the
  output is extractive by construction — no rapidfuzz alignment needed.
- The minima-based rule is a heuristic local-extremum detector with a single
  threshold θ. It is a natural baseline to replace with a principled change
  point method (PELT / penalised cost) over the same series — see
  `ideas/surprisal-cpd-boundaries.md`.
- Context handling matters: PPL is conditioned on the full preceding text, and
  §3.3 argues longer context lowers PPL and helps. With llama.cpp we could get
  token logprobs for the whole document in one forward pass.

## Limitations

- Threshold θ and the minima rule are ad hoc; no analysis of sensitivity.
- No intrinsic segmentation evaluation (Pk / WD), only RAG QA.
- The theoretical section (§3.3) argues about cross-entropy and context length
  in general; it does not justify *why minima* specifically mark boundaries.
- Sentence-level mean PPL mixes sentence length and lexical rarity effects;
  no normalisation beyond the token average is discussed.

## Actionable ideas

- Reproduce PPL Chunking as a baseline boundary detector inside chunklabel
  (cheap: one forward pass with a small GGUF model via llama.cpp `logits_all`).
- Compare "minima rule" vs. PELT on the same series using Pk/WindowDiff
