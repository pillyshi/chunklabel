# Notes: kong-2015

> Kong, L., Dyer, C., Smith, N. A. (2015). Segmental Recurrent Neural Networks.
> arXiv:1511.06018. Published at *ICLR 2016*.

## Summary

Segmental RNNs (SRNNs) are a semi-CRF (`sarawagi-2004.md`) whose segment scores come
from neural networks. Every candidate segment (contiguous span) is encoded by a
forward and a backward RNN into a "segment embedding", which is combined with the
label and segment length to give a score. A global semi-Markov CRF combines these
scores. The paper names the task "joint segmentation and labeling" / "segmental
labeling": break the input into contiguous, arbitrary-length segments while labeling
each one.

## Model and inference

- Output is a sequence of labels `y_i ∈ Y` (fixed set) with durations `z_i`, and
  `Σ z_i = |x|`: segments **cover the whole input without overlap**.
- Segment score: `w^T φ(V [g_y(y); g_z(z); RNN_fwd(span); RNN_bwd(span)] + a) + b`.
- All `O(|x|^2)` span embeddings are computed in `O(|x|^2)` by extending spans one
  token at a time; an upper bound on segment length reduces this to `O(|x|)`.
- MAP segmentation and the partition function use the same dynamic program
  (`O(|x|^2 · |Y|)`), run in log space to avoid overflow.

## Training

- **Fully supervised**: both boundaries and labels observed.
- **Partially supervised**: only the label sequence is observed; boundaries are latent
  and marginalized (sum over segmentations consistent with the labels).

## Experiments

- Online handwriting recognition (Kassel 1995): SRNNs reach 2.7–5.4% test error vs
  13.8% for CTC. The partially supervised model did best on test (different writers),
  suggesting latent boundaries can generalize better.
- Joint Chinese word segmentation and POS tagging (CTB5): SRNNs beat a BiLSTM BIO
  tagger (test F_seg 95.5 vs 95.0, F_tag 90.0 vs 88.3). Pure word segmentation
  (SIGHAN 2005): better on 3 of 4 portions.

## Key findings relevant to chunklabel

- **Same problem as chunklabel**, with a supervised, fixed-label-set setting. The
  paper's related-work section describes BIO tagging and CTC as reductions of the
  "segmental" problem to token-level sequence labeling; this supports describing
  chunklabel's task as segmentation-and-labeling rather than as sequence labeling or
  span classification.
- **Score every span, then decode**: SRNNs score each candidate span independently and
  let a DP pick a consistent segmentation. This is the same structure as span-based
  NER and could be applied to chunklabel by scoring sentence-level spans with an LLM
  instead of an RNN.
- **Latent boundaries**: partially supervised training shows boundaries can be inferred
  from labels alone. This matters for evaluation datasets where only section labels
  are known (e.g. `dernoncourt-2017.md`).

## Limitations

- Supervised, fixed label set; nothing about open-vocabulary labels.
- Quadratic in sequence length without a segment length bound; tested on short
  sequences (words of strokes, sentences of characters), far shorter than chunklabel
  inputs.
- No Markov dependency between adjacent labels in the experiments (zeroth order).
