# Notes: vinh-2010

> Vinh, N. X., Epps, J., Bailey, J. (2010). Information Theoretic Measures for
> Clusterings Comparison: Variants, Properties, Normalization and Correction for
> Chance. JMLR 11, 2837–2854.
> Read together with [rosenberg-2007] (V-measure).

## Summary

Systematic study of mutual-information-based measures for comparing two clusterings
(NMI variants, VI, NID), and of **adjustment for chance** (AMI), with comparisons to
the Rand index and ARI.

## Key findings

- Unadjusted measures (RI, MI, NMI and its variants, which include V-measure) have a
  **non-constant baseline**: comparing random clusterings to a fixed "truth", the score
  rises monotonically with the number of clusters K. A random 7-cluster solution
  tends to beat a random 3-cluster one (§4, Fig. 1).
- ARI and the proposed AMI have a baseline close to 0 regardless of K.
- The bias matters mainly when N/K is small. When `N/K ≥ 100`, the expected mutual
  information is close to 0 and adjustment is "not of utmost necessity" (§4, Thm 7).
- Trade-off: the adjusted measures are not metrics. NID is recommended as a general
  choice when the metric property matters.

From [rosenberg-2007]: V-measure = harmonic mean of homogeneity
(`1 − H(C|K)/H(C)`, each cluster contains a single class) and completeness
(`1 − H(K|C)/H(K)`, each class is in a single cluster). It needs no matching between
cluster names and classes.

## Key findings relevant to chunklabel

- chunklabel's free-form labels can be scored against gold labels as a **clustering
  comparison**: assign every character (or token) its gold label and its predicted
  label, then compare the two partitions. No mapping between label names is needed.
  This evaluates segmentation and labeling together.
- **Granularity bias is exactly the issue**: a more fine-grained chunking means more
  predicted clusters, which inflates unadjusted NMI/V-measure. Use ARI or AMI, or
  report homogeneity and completeness separately (they move in opposite directions
  with granularity).
- At character level, N per document is thousands and K is around 5–20, so N/K ≥ 100
  usually holds and the bias is mild. At sentence level (N ≈ 30–60), it is not, and
  adjustment is needed.

## Limitations

- Treats data points as exchangeable. It ignores contiguity, so two same-label chunks
  far apart count as one cluster. That is correct for "label consistency" but is a
  different notion from segmentation.
- Character-level evaluation weights long chunks more than short ones.
